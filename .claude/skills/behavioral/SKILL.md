# Behavioral

Folder: [behavioral/](../../../behavioral/) — stories in
[behavioral/stories/](../../../behavioral/stories/), per-company prep in
[behavioral/company-notes/](../../../behavioral/company-notes/) · template:
[templates/behavioral-star.md](../../../templates/behavioral-star.md)

The strategy is a **library of 6–8 stories**, not an answer per question. Prompts get mapped onto
stories, so coverage matters more than polish.

## Scope

STAR-structured stories · the coverage matrix in
[behavioral/README.md](../../../behavioral/README.md) · "tell me about yourself" (90 seconds,
past → present → why here) · "why this firm / this desk" · questions to ask the interviewer ·
failure and conflict stories · handling salary and timeline questions · firm-specific values
mapping · reference-check-safe framing (nothing you wouldn't want repeated).

## Standards for a story

- **Situation and Task: 25% of the airtime.** Enough for a stranger; no org charts, no acronyms.
- **Action: 60%**, in **first person singular**. What *you* decided, what you traded off, whom you
  brought in. Bulleted in the file so it can be compressed or expanded live.
- **Result: 15%**, with a number where one exists. "Cut p99 from 800 ms to 120 ms" beats
  "improved performance a lot".
- **Plus one honest line** on what you'd do differently — interviewers probe here, and having it
  ready converts a weakness question into a strength.
- **Two minutes spoken.** Time it out loud; written stories always run long.
- **Tagged with every prompt it answers**, so one story covers three questions.

## Coverage — the gaps are the work

Every empty row in the matrix is a question that will be asked eventually: hardest technical
problem, failure, conflict with a manager or peer, leading without authority, ambiguity,
prioritization under deadline, persuading with data, receiving hard feedback, something you'd
redo. When a row is empty, the job is to find the real experience that fills it — not to invent
one.

## Hard rule when tutoring this

**Never invent experience.** You may ask questions, restructure what the user tells you, tighten
their wording, and point out what's missing — you may not supply facts, results, or numbers they
didn't give you. A fabricated story fails a reference check and collapses under a follow-up.

If a matrix row is empty, ask questions that surface a real candidate ("when did a project of
yours slip?"), don't draft a plausible story for them to adopt.

## Traps to catch

- **"We" instead of "I"** — the most common failure. It hides the user's own contribution, and
  interviewers read it as having none.
- All Situation, no Action: three minutes of context, then "and it worked out".
- A failure story with no lesson, or one where the failure is secretly someone else's fault.
- A conflict story where the user is simply right and the other person unreasonable — that reads
  as poor self-awareness. Best conflict stories show a changed mind.
- Blaming a former employer or teammate.
- No numbers anywhere.
- A "weakness" that's a humblebrag ("I care too much").
- Rambling past the answer; not stopping when done.
- "Why this firm" that would apply to any firm — it must contain one concrete detail about them.
- No questions for the interviewer, or questions answered on their careers page.

## Practice format

Four prompts, two minutes each, out loud, no notes, no restarts. Then critique: STAR balance,
I-vs-we, whether a number appeared, whether they answered the question actually asked. Record the
session under [mock-interviews/](../../../mock-interviews/) with
[templates/mock-interview.md](../../../templates/mock-interview.md).

## Company notes

One file per firm in `behavioral/company-notes/`: their stated values with a story mapped to each,
the loop structure and who you'll meet, your why-this-firm answer, three questions for them, and
your compensation/timeline position. Keep it factual — if you don't know their loop, write that
rather than guessing.
